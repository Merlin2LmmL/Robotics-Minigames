Editor

Color and Pushable change the selected shape, or the next one drawn when nothing is selected. Lines can't be pushed, and pushed polygons turn when hit off-center. Delete removes the selection, Ctrl+Z undoes, the mouse wheel zooms and right-drag pans.

Turn the editor off to drive the sandbox map. Maps are saved in this browser and can be exported as .json files; importing one loads it into the sandbox.

Zoom

Environment

On draws the walls and obstacles. Off hides them, leaving only the lidar beams and their hit points.

Drive

Hold a button to drive. Keyboard WASD and arrow keys work too.

The IMU measures acceleration (right is positive sideways) and turn rate, both with noise and a small constant bias; the wheels report forward speed with noise and a small wheel size error. The purple outline is where the robot would think it is using only the gyro and the wheels, and it drifts further from the truth the longer you drive. It calibrates the gyro during its first 2 s of standing still, which removes most of the bias; the wheel size error and wall scrapes still add up.

Position error over the last 30 s, with the RMS error in meters. Vertical lines mark sensor switches.

      
An extended Kalman filter predicts the motion from the accelerometer, then corrects it with each measurement in proportion to how much it trusts it. The gyro and wheels alone still drift, just slower than dead reckoning. The lidar compares every few beams with the distance the map predicts from the estimated pose and pulls the estimate back to where the walls fit; beams that hit people or pallets don't fit and are rejected. GPS never drifts but jumps around by a meter; fused with the rest it stops jumping. The green ellipse is where the filter is 95% sure the robot is. Scramble throws the estimate off to watch it recover.

The robot draws its own map from the lidar, without knowing the real one: white is floor it has seen, dark is wall, gray is unexplored. Turn the environment off to see only what it knows. Its position comes from odometry corrected by scan matching, and when it comes back to a place it has been before, the loop is closed and the whole path (and the map with it) gets pulled straight. Drive a big loop and watch it snap. The pose graph shows the keyframes in orange and the loop closures in pink; the orange outline is where SLAM thinks the robot is. The map starts after 2 s of standing still, while the gyro calibrates.
